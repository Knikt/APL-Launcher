# Interaction behaviour — 0.6.1

## Home

- Dock: Left/Right scrolls the apps without wrapping. Up from the first row focuses the time pill in the top-right capsule; the capsule widens to show the two places it leads, a sliders icon for the control panel and a house icon for the Home tab, while the clock stays as its label. Left/Right arms one of the two and OK opens it; Right past the house icon reaches the round Search pill, Left comes back, and Down or Back returns to the selected app. That round pill carries the magnifier again and opens Search on OK or a click; the same Search door also sits in the Control Centre beside Art Mode, Smart Home and Settings. OK on the time pill, or a click on it, only widens the capsule, so the Home tab is reachable from the pill itself. The control panel opens under the capsule as a two-column grid of 188 px cells with 12 px gutters, so the panel is 424 px square: the day and date on one line, a square switch one cell wide and two rows high with a Wi-Fi capsule and a Bluetooth capsule one cell each stacked beside it, a full-width weather capsule (two cells and the gutter) below them, then four round buttons - Art Mode, Smart Home, Settings and Search - splitting that same width four ways. The switch is the unit: Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are exactly its width, the weather capsule is twice that width, and each round button is a quarter of it. There is no volume tile; the set's own remote already owns volume. Up/Down walks that grid (Wi-Fi above Bluetooth, then the weather capsule and the round row), Left/Right stays inside a row, Up from the top row or Left from the switch returns to the capsule, OK acts and Back closes it. The time pill and the round Search pill still share one glass capsule, so the top-right reads as a single object, and the armed tool carries the same white plate as a focused tile. The top-left status header is gone: the time pill is the only clock on the Dock. While the drawer is showing rows below the first, the capsule and its panel fade out, leaving the upper area intentionally empty. They return at row 0.
- Home tab: the house icon in the capsule opens the Home Assistant devices in the same top-right slot, with the same separate glass tiles as the control panel. There is no room selector: every device the private client lists sits in one grid, deduplicated by entity id and ordered by card size - air conditioners first, then fans, then speakers - so the mosaic fills without holes. Home uses the Control Centre's 424px sheet width, 188px columns, 88px rows and 12px gutters: an air conditioner is a 188px square spanning two rows, a fan is a 188x88 half card, and a speaker takes the full 388px row. Every control is drawn on the card itself - no second pane, so the sheet never changes size. An air conditioner shows its reading in the middle of the square with the state tag above it, and its three keys on the lower arc: cooler at one shoulder, warmer at the other, power in the middle and a step lower, so the switch is between the two temperature keys rather than above them. A fan or speaker card puts its mark in the left corner as the switch, its name and state beside it, and a whitish translucent fill across the card that shows fan speed or speaker volume; a running fan's mark wears the blue accent and drops back to plain ink once it stops. The focused card keeps its frozen slice and adds a slightly brighter translucent pane; every card carries one light white glint along the top edge, with no bright outline around the rest of it. All four arrows walk the grid, and it never wraps. Up and Down step in reading order from the first square to the last bar; Left and Right move to the nearest card in that direction, measured from the cards' own boxes so a wide speaker and a square air conditioner behave the same. A card's own keys and its bar still step its value directly - half a degree on an air conditioner, one speed step on a fan, five percent on a speaker's volume - so no one needs an arrow to change a setting. OK, a click on the card, or a click on its mark switches the device: an air conditioner between cooling and off, a fan between on and off, a speaker between playing and paused. Clicking a bar jumps straight to that point on it, and clicking the air conditioner's minus or plus keys steps the set point. Back closes the sheet, and it scrolls inside itself when the devices outgrow the space above the Dock. The sheet only reads and writes states the private Home Assistant client already tracks; the public build ships an empty room list and shows a short "not set up" note instead.
- Ask: the spark in the capsule drops the assistant's sheet from the top edge over a dimmed stage. The sheet is one glass surface at all times, 900 px wide, centred on the stage: no wait for a full-screen transition. Holding the remote's mic key - where the firmware delivers it to an app at all - or holding any of the four colour keys, which it always does, drops the same sheet as a single listening orb - the light answers, the room is heard - and releasing it opens the app's own keyboard with whatever was heard, so a misheard word can be corrected before the question is sent; holding the mic again from the keyboard or from an answer returns to listening. Where the platform gives the app no speech service but does allow the microphone, the hold records a short clip and the model transcribes it into the same keyboard, and a set with neither still opens the keyboard, so the assistant is never unreachable. An answer grows the same sheet downward with the response, any web sources the model grounded on sit under it as small pills, and the answer is read aloud: the set's own speech engine is tried first, and if it never starts - or the set has none - the same text comes back as Gemini audio, played once behind the sheet. Closing the sheet or holding the mic stops whatever is still being spoken, and a set where both voices fail says once that the answer stays on screen. A plain household command - on, off, a fan's percentage, an air conditioner's set point, a volume, the TV's settings or its power - is matched locally and answered on the frame as a confirmation badge that fades out by itself, without a model round trip. Up/Down scrolls a long answer, OK starts another question, and Back closes the sheet from any state. While the sheet is open the keys belong to it: the dim behind it closes it on a click, and the stage's top-right cluster steps out of the way so the top edge reads as one surface.
- The assistant is given the screen's live state with every question - the view, the layout, the focused app, the backdrop's title and year, and the home's devices - so "what is this show about" answers about the title on the wallpaper rather than a guess. Its orb is one WebGL shader rendered at half resolution and stopped whenever the sheet is hidden; the sheet's frost is switched on only after the drop has settled, so the TV never blurs a moving layer.
- List: Up/Down scrolls the apps without wrapping. Right enters the bottom-right controls, aligned with the compact clock above. Up/Down moves between Art Mode, Smart Home and Settings. Left or Back returns to the selected app. The list fades at both ends as it scrolls. The round Search pill stays top-centre above the list, on its own plate; the time pill belongs to the Dock and is hidden in List.
- On the Dock, the first row swaps the ground for a title backdrop from the focused app. Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar, Apple TV and Sony LIV draw from JustWatch's trending list for the storefront, filtered by that provider's real offers; Stremio shares the storefront's trending list, and Nuvio draws Netflix's picks; YouTube uses bundled artwork. Any ground carries it — Photo, Dark and Light alike — while the List layout keeps its own background. Scrolling the drawer below the first row freezes the poster that was showing when the scroll started and brings a pre-rendered soft copy of it in front, the same cached-canvas technique as the Control Center's field rather than a live filter; the frozen poster and its title card survive the trip and return exactly as they were, so the drawer reads over the same backdrop instead of dropping back to the ground. One backdrop is shown at a time and a change cross-fades in about 320 ms between two layers, with no live blur. For top-row focus changes, the poster waits until its panel-glass blur image is decoded; then the poster and pill glass start their matching cross-fades together. The row's images are fetched ahead of the move that needs them, so a scroll changes the poster on the keypress rather than after a download. They are fetched one at a time, the focused app's first and the rest of the row behind it: six 1080p decodes requested together were a burst big enough to swallow the first presses after start, and each poster now waits only for its own stage-scale frost rather than for the finer pane tile as well. Only the last six posters and their frosts are kept: a tile left in focus walks its app's titles, and pinning every 1920x1080 bitmap of a session is what made the set run out of memory while the app sat behind another one - and be killed rather than resumed on the way back. A top-row tile left in focus for ten seconds does not hold one title: the held poster slides out to the left while the next title of that same app slides in from the right, and the app's list keeps walking for as long as the viewer stays on it. The timer follows the focus rather than the clock - moving along the row, opening the capsule's panels, scrolling below the first row or hiding the window disarms it, and re-rendering the same tile never postpones it. The next title starts downloading the moment the dwell arms, so the slide lands on the tick rather than when a cold fetch finishes. The poster is decoded before the move is allowed to start, and the rest of the UI is refreshed only once the pair has landed, so neither a cold JPEG decode nor a full render falls on a frame of the slide. The rotation only picks titles no other tile on the row is showing, and bundled artwork, which has no catalogue behind it, simply holds.
- Each app on screen is given a different title for the session, because the same title sits near the top of several provider lists and a shared image would make a move look like nothing happened. Every app also keeps its own rotation cursor that advances once per launch, and the catalogue is refetched when it is older than six hours, so a later start or a new release opens on a different title. Artwork is fetched at 1440 wide - the smallest backdrop this host serves, a third smaller than the 1920 rendition and just over half the pixels to decode - and the profile travels with the stored catalogue, so a build that asks for a different width discards the old one instead of showing posters whose addresses no longer resolve. A failed image clears the title card and returns the ground rather than leaving the previous app's poster on screen, which would read as every tile sharing one backdrop. Backdrops are decorative: the app name stays in the focused tile's label.
- The focused backdrop shows its title top left in the Home font, set large, uppercase and light on the poster, with the year beneath it. YouTube's bundled artwork carries no title, so nothing is written there. The title follows the same fade the top bar uses and disappears with it when the drawer leaves the first row.
- OK opens the selected item. Focus alone never launches an app, and a focused Dock tile has no white ring; it lifts, scales and glows in the app's own colour, with its name below the card. The lift springs: the card overshoots by a hair and settles onto its resting size. The same slight bounce runs wherever focus lands - control and Home tiles, the capsule's buttons, search keys, scope pills, suggestions and cards, and the sheet's rows and buttons - 320 ms for a pop and 360 ms for the lift, transform only, and every pop ends back at the size that element's own rules give it, so losing focus never snaps.
- Icons-only apps and controls reveal their names when focused. App presentation and control presentation are independent.
- Back from the Dock opens a choice to stay or open Samsung Home.
- Rearranging the Dock: press and hold OK on an app tile and the dock enters arrange mode, the way an iPad's home screen does. Every tile starts a slow wobble - a fraction of a degree, each column on its own stagger, transform-only - while the app that was held stops wobbling and floats above the row, larger and clear of its neighbours. Left/Right walk the held app through the row while the rest slide out of the way, and the new order is written as it moves. OK sets it down there and the dock stays in arrange mode, so the next app can be picked up; picking up again is the same OK. Back sets down a held app, and Back again leaves arrange mode. A tap is unchanged - OK still opens the app on release - so nothing moves unless the key is actually held. Membership (adding and removing apps) stays in Settings, under Apps on Home.
- Scroll motion follows speed and direction. A single press is a step and keeps the soft 380 ms settle; a repeat under 200 ms is read as a scroll, and while it lasts the row runs one short settle from the side the viewer came from (Left/Right for the row, Up/Down for the drawer), drops the artwork's parallax and keeps to transform and opacity. The class that arms it sits on `body`, which no render pass rewrites, and it clears 260 ms after the last press, so coming to rest restores the soft motion. The state of that scroll is not remembered across a restart.
- Dock artwork ships at 320x200 for a card drawn at 288x200 (317 px wide while focused): the earlier 504x316 tiles were oversampled, and the smaller ones cut the bytes and the pixels the set decodes as a row comes into range without changing how a tile looks.
- A panel keeps focus within its own controls. Back closes one panel at a time.
- Returning to the running launcher keeps the selected app or panel item. There is no auto-return timer. Held OK and Back keys act once until the key is released.

## Search

- Up from the first Dock row focuses the time pill; Right reaches the Search pill and OK there opens the full-screen Search view. Back closes it. The sheet fades and rises into place over about 190 ms, and stays visible for the full close transition before it is hidden. It is laid out like the set's own search page in the reference: the blurred artwork behind is the page itself, under one scrim (no card, no panel), with the magnifier and the query as the search bar across the top and a small spinner at the right while the catalogue answers. The keyboard owns the left column and the right column carries the `Search In:` row over a hairline, then the title rows. Focus scrolling is immediate, and the stage behind the sheet is not rebuilt while search has focus, so a keypress moves focus in about a millisecond on the development machine.
- The query is typed on the page's own keyboard and appears in that top row, right beside the magnifier — the field is the search bar, so nothing is typed into a separate box. Nothing on this screen is a text input, so the TV's on-screen keyboard never opens over it: the query is drawn with a caret and the magnifier stays as its mark. `abc` and `123` are the two layouts, switched with Left/Right on the segmented control; the letter rows are a–f, g–l, m–r, s–x and y–z, with one utility row of space, delete and clear beneath them. No key is a box until it is selected: a focused key is a solid white square with dark type, and so is the focused segment of the layout switch. A keypress appends to the query (up to 48 characters) and starts a short debounce before the catalogue is asked. Right past the end of a key row and Down from the utility row enter the results.
- `Search In:` lists the installed apps that can take a term — Apple TV, YouTube, Netflix, Prime Video and JioHotstar first, whichever are installed, four pills wide. Left/Right walks the pills, OK closes the page and opens that app's own search with the typed term, Up returns to the query and Down enters the results. This is the same route `FrameDevice.search` takes for a title card's offer, so the term lands in that app's results rather than on its home screen.
- While typing, a short suggestion list sits under the `Search In:` row and answers on the keypress: the closest title the catalogue has offered, an installed app whose name matches, then a row for an app that can take the term. The app rows need no network, so the list is never empty once a letter is typed. Left/Right from the keyboard reaches it, Up returns to the scope row, Down enters the results, and OK on a title opens that title, on an app opens the app, and on an app row hands the term to that app's search.
- Underneath, results are landscape 16:9 cards three across — TV Shows above Movies, both sections drawn before typing as well, when the catalogue answers with what is popular. The service mark and the title sit under the still frame rather than over it, so nothing covers the artwork and the name is readable without focusing; the chosen card adds a white ring. A movie or show card opens that title in its streaming app through the offer's own title page and ids — Prime Video and its GTI payload, Apple TV, and Nuvio and Stremio through their imdb detail routes, are the confirmed exact-title launches on the reference TV. A Netflix offer is handed to Nuvio instead: this set has no working Netflix app, and Nuvio opens the same title from its IMDb id. SonyLIV and JioHotstar take their id rungs first, as the chain below describes; the other apps receive the title page and search as fallbacks. A card with no confirmed offer is removed once availability has loaded; it is not sent to Nuvio or another library app by default. Home Assistant is intentionally not part of Search; it lives in the Home tab of the top-right capsule.
- Searching inside an app is not just a launch. `FrameDevice.search` sends the term to YouTube's own results route, to any provider with a verified route (Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar, Apple TV, Sony LIV, Disney+, Stremio, Nuvio, Apple Music and Samsung TV Plus) through the same controls a title card uses, and to any other installed app through Tizen's search operation with the term as application-control text, falling back to a default-operation payload and then a plain launch. An app that answers none of those still opens, and only the query is lost.
- Availability is resolved from JustWatch's public GraphQL offers for the current country. The country comes from the weather location when available, then the TV locale or time zone. The provider badge therefore reflects a real regional offer rather than a title-name guess. Offers are matched by IMDb id first, then title and year, then title within the same release year (or one year either side for regional releases). A title-only match is rejected when the years are further apart, so a 2005 film called *Severance* cannot inherit the 2022 Apple TV show's offer; a card with no year is accepted only when just one same-title offer exists. Until availability answers, a card carries no badge at all. When availability finishes without an offer, the card is removed rather than attributed to a local library. Results are ordered by the services the owner browses first: Netflix, JioHotstar, Apple TV, Sony LIV, Disney+ and Prime Video. Amazon third-party channels such as `amazonappletvplus` and `amazonsonyliv` belong to Prime Video, not to the standalone app that shares their brand. JustWatch can be slow on a cold query; artwork appears first, then the badges are patched in place when availability arrives rather than rebuilding the cards.
- Prime Video is the only third-party title payload verified on the reference TV. Its GTI is taken from the JustWatch offer URL and sent as `{"values":"amzn1.dv.gti..."}` in the `PAYLOAD` application-control data, using the default operation and no URI. The owner confirmed that the exact title opens.
- YouTube search first sends the normal results address (`https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=...`) through a view control, then Tizen's standard search operation with `http://tizen.org/appcontrol/data/text`, and finally opens the app normally. The suggestions' YouTube row and a title card's YouTube offer both use this route, and TizenTube or YouTube TV resolve to the same one with their own package. Direct video URLs use their own view control when a video ID is known; Search cannot obtain those IDs from YouTube's cross-origin endpoint, so Search no longer lists video cards. The results-page route still needs visible confirmation on the reference TV.
- A provider's chain tries one rung at a time and only moves on when the previous one is refused: the offer's title page through the `view` operation, the bare link under the universal-search wrapper (`PAYLOAD` with `values` as a plain string, the shape Samsung's Smart Hub Preview documents), the JSON `PAYLOAD`, then the app's own search control, with a plain launch last. Apple TV adds its own scheme (`com.apple.tv://<type>/<id>`) and its id payload before the JSON wrapper, seven rungs in all, and the owner confirmed on 29 October that it opens the exact title. A provider whose offer carries no page still gets its own address built from the id (`/title/<id>`, `/in/watch/<id>`, `/detail/<id>`), so the control never falls back to a search URL while an id is known.
- SonyLIV and JioHotstar go the other way round, because a link handed to either one opens its home screen and the launch reports success, so every rung after it is skipped. Their id rungs run first: every numeric id on the offer page is offered in turn, show id before episode id, under the names their packages advertise (`assetId`, `contentId`, `id`) and as the JSON `PAYLOAD`; then their own scheme (`sonyliv://content/<id>`, `hotstar://content/<id>`), the bare wrapper, the title page, the JSON wrapper, their search screen and a plain launch. SonyLIV answered "content not found" to the ids from its own pages in the 26 October test, which means it does route by id; whether the id-first order lands a title is unconfirmed on the set. JioHotstar still opens its app home for a title, and the launcher makes no direct-title claim for either until the owner sees one.
- Samsung Universal Search has no public third-party search API. Its UMD feed carries provider-defined `deeplink_payload` data, which the TV passes to the selected app as `PAYLOAD`; the payload schema belongs to each provider. CleanFrame implements the one schema that could be verified (Prime Video GTI) and keeps honest app-search fallbacks for the rest.
- If the availability service is unavailable, cards keep only the provider they already had and no new Nuvio fallback is invented. Focus alone never launches a result.

## Settings and appearance

Settings opens a grouped overview: Background, Layout, Typography, Focus, Apps on Home and Samsung. OK opens a group. Appearance groups use a two-pane screen with visible choices on the left and the committed Home preview on the right.

| Group | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Background | Photo, Light, Dark; Wallpaper and Rotation when Photo is active |
| Layout | List or Dock; independent Text, Icons or Both choices for apps and Home controls |
| Typography | Manrope, Neue Montreal, Mori, Pangram Sans, Geist and Newsreader, when available |
| Focus | Type or Glass; Soft, Frost or Clear tint material |
| Apps on Home | Membership and arrangement editor |
| Screen Saver | Aerial theme (Landscape, Space, Underwater, Cityscapes, My Photos); start after 1, 2, 5 or 10 minutes, or never; Preview |
| Samsung | Samsung Home and TV settings |

Up/Down moves between rows, and the list follows the focus in the same frame: the rows themselves are not animated, so holding a D-pad down a long group - the screen saver's video list - keeps the focused row inside the visible box instead of leaving the highlight ahead of a list that is still gliding after it. Left/Right moves between choices in a horizontal row. Focus alone changes neither the preview nor saved preferences in these appearance groups. Wallpaper has its own temporary photo preview, described below. OK applies the focused value. A checkmark identifies the applied value; the filled plate identifies remote focus. Applying the current value does not write again.

Material applies to a photographic Dock, or Glass focus on a photographic List. Inactive material choices remain visible with an explanation and are skipped during navigation. Their stored value is retained. Wallpaper and rotation are also inactive on flat backgrounds.

A font or photo change applies only after its resources are ready and storage succeeds. A load or storage failure keeps the previous appearance and checkmark, with a Try again action. Back cancels a pending change, leaves the group and keeps completed changes. Late load callbacks cannot apply a cancelled choice. There is no Save button. Returning to a group restores its last choice focus; returning to Settings restores its category focus. Rotation is nested within Background, and Back returns to its Rotation row.

The selected font applies to Home app names, the clock and the time pill, including Newsreader. Settings, the Home editor and status text retain Manrope. The preview shows the committed Home appearance and its clock continues to update. Pangram faces are available in Nathan's private build when the local files are present; their binaries are not included in the public source. An unavailable optional face is omitted. If the baseline font is slow or missing, Home remains usable with a local fallback and can recover when the font loads.

The Home controls choice (Icons, Icons and text, Text) describes the List's bottom-right column. The Dock keeps one time pill that opens the control panel and, through the widened capsule, the Home tab; its capsules always show an icon with a label, and its round buttons are icon-only with the name as their accessible label, so changing that choice never removes a Dock control.

## Streaming apps

The bundled apps keep their known native IDs. Other installed consumer apps also appear in the drawer. Launching uses the public `tizen.application.launch` API with the app's native ID. Search launches a real provider offer and can pass a verified title payload to Prime Video; YouTube opens its search page, and other providers try their own title/search controls before falling back to the app. CleanFrame still exits itself whenever another app opens — a persistent web-app home makes Tizen treat it as a browser and lock picture modes — but a launch only reports acceptance, and on a cold start the target window does not exist for several seconds. Losing focus happens almost immediately when the launch is accepted, so it is not proof that the target is ready: exiting then leaves the home role momentarily ownerless and Tizen restarts CleanFrame over the app that is still opening (first launch bounces back, second launch works). Every launch path therefore waits 7 seconds after CleanFrame leaves the front before exiting, cancels that exit if CleanFrame comes back to the front (the target failed or the viewer returned Home), and keeps a 20-second ceiling as a last resort. The owner confirmed the cold first-launch bounce is fixed on the reference TV. Missing apps and denied launches produce a real error. No web player, placeholder success page or account credential is embedded.

## App drawer

The dock is the full app drawer. It reads `tizen.application.getAppsInfo` at startup and lists every installed consumer app in six-column rows, alphabetically after the chosen Home order. A private build puts CleanFrame's own **Home** tile first, before every installed app: a blue plate with a white house, drawn by the launcher rather than installed as a package, and marked `builtin` so the installed-app filter never removes it. A public build has no tile at all, because `CleanFrameHA.enabled` is false in its stub. OK on it opens the Home app (see below) over the stage; no browser is involved and the launcher never leaves the screen.

Row marks are built one row past the focused one. A row further down is drawn as its plate, with no artwork, until it comes within that range and is filled in; nothing visible is ever blank, and the launcher does not load every icon in the drawer before its first frame. Samsung's visibility flag alone also exposes system utilities, so EP packages, background services, ad players, account utilities, placeholders and duplicates are omitted. Known brands use bundled marks. Other apps use their local installed icon, with initials as a fallback.

The locked grid keeps every row on the same columns and centres the six-column block; a short final row fills from the first column rather than forming a pyramid. Up/Down pages the rows without wrapping, so the drawer can hold every installed app. Scrolling below the first row fades the top UI away and freezes the current poster behind a soft full-stage copy of itself; the area stays empty while the app rows have the screen. OK launches an app. An API failure keeps saved Home choices. Browser previews report that installed-app discovery and launching require the TV.

## Home app

The Home tile opens the Home app — a screen of CleanFrame's own, drawn from the Home app on an iPad, not a browser tab and not the Control Centre's device sheet. The home's photograph fills the screen behind a translucent pane: the sidebar holds `Home` first, then `Categories` (Climate, Lights, Fans, Speakers & TVs) and `Rooms` (every room the private client lists). Up/Down walks the sidebar past its section headings, OK or Right selects the entry and moves into the tiles, and Left out of the first column comes back. The heading names what the pane is showing, a row of status capsules under it summarises climate range, lights on, speakers playing and fans on, and the pane itself is `Scenes` and then one section per room; a category shows one section, a room shows only its own accessories.

Tiles are the accessory: an air conditioner takes a 2x2 square with its reading large at the bottom, a speaker spans two columns, everything else is a square. A tile that is on turns white with dark type; a tile that is off stays glass over the photograph, exactly as the reference does, and an unavailable entity fades without pretending to have a state. Because the tiles are not one size, a D-pad move is resolved from their geometry — the nearest tile in that direction wins, with the cross axis weighted so the tile directly above or below beats one further along the row. OK on a tile opens the sheet at the bottom right with that accessory's own controls: power, temperature and mode for an air conditioner, speed for a fan, brightness for a lamp, playback and volume for a speaker, the scene's Run for a scene. Up/Down walks the rows, Left/Right steps the focused row, and OK acts on it. Back leaves the app from anywhere inside it, sheet or not.

The app reads and writes only what the private Home Assistant client already tracks, and it asks that client for its faster cadence while it is open. It adds no live blur - the backdrop is a pre-softened copy of a bundled photograph and every surface is flat alpha - so a press in it costs class flips rather than raster work. The public build has no client, so it has no tile.

## Apps on Home

Open the editor from Settings → Apps on Home. On Home shows the current order on the left. Not on Home shows Recently removed first, followed by other installed apps on the right. Home holds one to twenty apps.

- Reorder: OK on an app name picks it up. Up/Down moves it. OK places it and saves the order. Back puts an unplaced app back without saving that move.
- Remove: Right from an app name reaches Remove from Home. OK saves the removal and moves focus to the same app in Recently removed. The app stays installed.
- Add: OK on an app in Not on Home adds it and saves. A recently removed app returns to its previous position within the editing session; another app is added at the end. Focus follows the app to its Remove from Home control, so another OK can reverse the addition.
- Undo reverses a completed addition, removal or reorder from the current editing session. Done or Back closes the editor and keeps completed changes. Undo history ends when the editor closes.

Removing the last Home app or adding a twenty-first app is prevented. A failed storage write keeps the committed choices and order, with a retry message. Saved native app IDs and names survive a restart or a failed catalogue refresh.

## Art Mode and Samsung screens

Art Mode sends an application control to the installed `org.tizen.art-app`, with `mode=fullscreen` and `requester=mobile`. This aims to enter native Art Mode with its current artwork, without toggling power or changing the selected artwork.

This mode comes from [research on Samsung's Art app](https://github.com/TheFab21/ha-samsungtv-smart/blob/master/notes/QN55LS03FAFXZA/ART_MODE_DECOMPILED.md), on another Tizen 9 Frame model. It is an internal Samsung contract, not a documented public Art API. Its visible behaviour on this exact firmware must be confirmed on the TV.

Samsung Home explicitly launches the original `com.samsung.tv.csfs` app, independently of the new Home assignment. The earlier `com.samsung.tv.coba.newhome` component did not open Smart Hub visibly and is no longer the escape target. TV settings still uses `com.samsung.tv.coba.setting`. Exact on-device destinations are recorded in docs/tv-status.md; launch callbacks alone are not visual proof. There is no blind power-key fallback.

All calls have bounded response handling. A failure leaves CleanFrame usable. No simulated device success views remain. Browser validation cannot confirm the visible Samsung destination.

## Preferences and wallpaper

The launcher stores appearance, Home order, recently removed apps, the last launched Home app, wallpaper and rotation locally. List and Dock share these preferences. The existing `cleanframe.home.v1` key is retained, with a fallback read from `cleanframe.shore.v1`. Legacy List maps to Dark + List + Type; Glass Dock and Glass Grid map to Photo + Dock + Glass. App order, wallpaper, rotation and the last Home app are preserved.

The gallery contains 15 bundled wallpapers. Settings → Background → Wallpaper keeps the two-pane Settings frame. Up/Down browses a vertical thumbnail list without wrapping; the right-hand Home preview shows the candidate while the surrounding Settings backdrop stays on the applied photo. OK applies the focused photo and stays in Wallpaper. The tick marks the applied photo and preview status distinguishes unsaved browsing. Back restores the latest applied photo, cancels pending loading and returns to Background’s Wallpaper row. Photo and material assets change as one complete bundle; a failed load restores the applied preview and offers retry. Preview loading waits briefly for remote focus to settle. OK during loading queues application for that photo; another browse move or Back cancels it. Browsing never saves, and repeated OK on the applied photo does not write again.

Settings → Background → Rotation offers Fixed, Each time Home opens, Every 5 minutes, Every 15 minutes and Every hour. Each time Home opens advances once when the launcher loads; a return to the already-running launcher does not advance it. Timed rotation counts only visible Photo Home time in either List or Dock. Panels, flat backgrounds and time spent in other apps pause the cycle. Changing the photo or rotation mode starts a new interval.

Launcher wallpaper is separate from native Art Mode. Runtime photos, materials and available fonts are local assets; they do not require the Mac or a network download.

## Clock and rendering

Both layouts show the TV's local time in 12-hour format, without AM/PM or seconds. It updates at the minute boundary and on return to the launcher. The Dock shows it in the top-right time pill and has no separate status header; the List keeps its compact right-aligned clock. No clock panel is shown.

The control panel carries the day and date (for example `Tuesday 27 October`) on one line above its tiles. Its weather capsule reads the local temperature and condition (such as `28° Clear`) with the city as its label, and OK refreshes the weather in place without closing the panel. The temperature comes from the local weather service; the Home Assistant climate sensor is not mixed into this tile. Weather comes from Open-Meteo using approximate IP location from ipwho.is, with geolocation-db.com as a fallback. The location and weather are cached locally; the country code also selects the JustWatch storefront. Without a temperature the capsule reads `Tap to update`, and the rest of the panel stays usable.

The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth capsules report what the TV exposes - the network name or connection type, and whether Bluetooth exists - and OK opens the TV's own screen for that list: the network list for Wi-Fi and the sound/speaker list for Bluetooth. If the TV refuses to launch that app for a widget, the request falls back to the main Settings screen. A private build reaches Settings by asking Home Assistant's Samsung TV remote to press the set's own Menu key: the firmware handles that key press, so it lands on the menu from whatever is on screen and no app sandbox can refuse it. Without that client the TV's own settings app is launched instead. A Tizen app cannot switch either radio itself, so the panel does not pretend to toggle them. A connected radio lights its own mark: the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth marks turn the reference's cyan when the TV reports a network or a Bluetooth capability, and that is the only colour on the panel.

The round Settings button opens this launcher's own Settings sheet, which is the only route to it in the Dock layout; the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth capsules are the routes to the TV's own screens. The square switch is the set's power: OK sends `turn_off` through the same private client and closes the panel, and a build without that client says so instead of pretending. No control-panel tile slides the full-screen launch card across the picture, because the TV's settings pane only covers a corner of the screen and the card would misdescribe what is happening; the request leaves immediately and the panel closes behind it. The marks are drawn large, as in the reference: 92 px on the switch, 44 px on the three capsules and 48 px on the round buttons, in white over the tile's frost. The focused tile deepens its glass and lights its rim instead of flipping to a white key, so the marks stay white and the selected tile is the darker pane; on the Light ground, whose ink is dark, the focused tile is the white key and the cyan darkens to stay legible.

The top-right capsule, its control panel and the Home tab show a blurred slice of the wallpaper behind them. Each tile is its own slice, registered to the tile's position on the stage, as described in [asset generation](assets.md), so the TV never runs a live backdrop blur and the frost lines up with the artwork behind that exact tile, including after the Home tab has scrolled. Each pane is the blurred artwork plus a hairline rim with only a trace of dark tint for legibility - not a white wash - so a surface keeps the colour of what is behind it. The capsule re-samples its slice whenever the focused app's poster changes and cross-fades it on the stage's own timing, so its frost changes with the artwork rather than after it. Every slice is sampled from layout offsets, not client rects: the capsule scales while a sheet opens or closes, and a rect sampled mid-transform left the frost a few pixels off once the bar was back. The time and Search pills are the two segments inside the capsule's one plate. Flat Dark and Light grounds use a matching translucent plate instead of a photograph, with the ink flipped to dark on the Light ground so the type and the capsule always agree.

While a title backdrop is showing, every field derived from the wallpaper steps aside, because that crop is no longer what is on screen: the pill and its control panel, the Home tab's tiles, the Dock's glass, the Settings sheet's panel image and Control Center's backdrop all drop theirs and sit directly over the poster, each keeping its own tint so their type stays readable. Settings, the Control Center and the Home tab therefore read as glass over the current backdrop rather than over the photo chosen in Settings.

UI sounds come from one Web Audio context and one decoded clip per sound, read the first time that sound is needed. Each clip ships as 24 kHz mono PCM WAV with its original OGG Vorbis kept as a fallback: the set's media stack did not decode the Vorbis these were authored as, which is why they were inaudible there while the browser played them; the two a viewer hears first - focus and select - are read once the launcher has settled, and nothing is read while the first frame is painted. A set with no Web Audio falls back to an `<audio>` element created on demand. The previous build opened twelve pools of three elements shortly after start, which the TV's media pipeline count does not survive: the sounds were silent on the set even though every file loaded in the browser.

Photographic materials and blurred panel backgrounds are generated before packaging and stay aligned with the full-screen image. Photo colour and contrast metadata are also prepared offline. Navigation does not sample image pixels, run an optics engine or apply live backdrop blur. Flat backgrounds use matching light or dark panel colours. Focus feedback uses transforms and opacity; reduced motion removes animated scaling and movement while retaining opacity feedback. Only the focused card runs the lift keyframes - the shadow plate and the glow transition to the same place instead - so a focus change carries one animation rather than three layers with large shadows. The control panel is built the first time it is opened, not at start.

## Screen Saver

Settings carries a Screen Saver group: a theme (Landscape, Space, Underwater, Cityscapes, My Photos), how long the set waits before it starts (1, 2, 5 or 10 minutes, or never), a Video list naming the clips that theme carries, and a Preview that fills the screen at once. Every choice is kept in `localStorage`, so the same gallery is waiting after a restart, and the Settings row reports it as the live value. The list is longer than the screen, so the rows move on the compositor as focus travels: the screen saver picker, its video list and the preview button are all reachable. Picking a video also picks its theme, and the chosen clip carries a tick. The module and its catalogue are read by `main.js` a couple of seconds after start instead of being parsed with the page, so the launcher's first frame does not carry 50 KB of screen-saver code; a Settings page opened in those first seconds fills its rows in as soon as the module lands.

The gallery fills the stage when the set has been left alone for that long and nothing else is open: the launcher on Home, no sheet, no Search, no Settings page. Any remote key brings the launcher straight back, and that key is spent doing it - it never also moves the focus behind the saver. Down is the exception: it asks for the caption, which brings the time and the place up in the bottom-right corner for eight seconds, as the Apple TV shows its own location line. Nothing is written over the footage until it is asked for.

Each theme is Apple's own aerial footage, streamed from Apple's servers rather than bundled: `assets/aerials/catalog.js` lists the clips per theme (Landscape, Space, Underwater, Cityscapes) and one is picked at random each time the gallery starts. The catalogue is generated from Apple's gallery by `scripts/build-aerial-catalog.py`, and its 113 addresses are the 1080p H.264 renditions, served over HTTP because Apple's HTTPS chain for that host is missing its issuing CA and cannot be verified. The clip's caption becomes the place tag. If the set cannot fetch or decode the container - the browser engine on the development machine has no H.264 at all - the sheet falls back within five seconds to a painted scene for the same theme: one 960x540 canvas stretched by a compositor transform, a quarter of the pixels, no video decode and no filter on the animation path, at 30 frames a second. Landscape drifts clouds and turns a horizon; Space carries a starfield, a nebula and the occasional streak; Underwater draws light shafts, caustics and motes; Cityscapes fades a sky behind lit windows; My Photos pushes slowly through the bundled gallery. The vignette and the corner tag's shadow are painted into the frame, not applied over it, and the gallery is never a black screen.

## Depth

A focused app tile keeps a flat card and turns the mark in front of it: the logo shifts against the tile by how far it sits from the middle of its row and takes a matching `rotateY`, so the card reads as a pane with the mark standing off it. One specular band crosses the glass as focus lands, on the same compositor layer. No tile carries `will-change`: on every card it kept forty layers with 70 px shadows alive, and on only the focused card it built a layer as focus arrived and threw it away as it left, which is churn on every move. A plain transform is composited when the frame needs it.

Motion across the launcher comes from one vocabulary: a gentle departure and a long weighted landing (`--ease`, `--ease-apple`, `--ease-apple-soft`), plus a soft overshoot (`--ease-spring`) for things that are picked up. Colour and opacity fades ride the same curves instead of stepping linearly, and the durations sit between 240 and 420 ms so nothing snaps. The capsule's frost is measured once the frame is painted rather than inside the key handler, because sampling it forced a layout read while the whole stage was dirty; that read was the several-millisecond spike on some moves.

The focused tile's own accent also tints the room: a static radial wash above the wallpaper takes `--ambient` from the tile's `--app-glow` and cross-fades by opacity when focus moves. Nothing is sampled from the poster and no filter runs while the colour changes.
